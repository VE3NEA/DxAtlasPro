Space Weather

The propagation and ionospheric maps show the ionosphere of a typical day of the month. The Space Weather panel tells you how today differs from that typical day: whether to expect better conditions than predicted on the high bands, or a storm that will close the polar paths. Click the Ionosphere button on the map toolbar and tick Space Weather to show the panel in the lower right corner of the map; the values come from the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center and update by themselves while the panel is shown.

Before You Get on the Air

Glance at the panel to decide which bands and which paths are worth trying:

  • SFI, the solar flux, tells you how high the bands go. Around 70, 15 and 10 m are seldom open beyond short skip; above 150, expect 10 m to open in daytime on long paths; the higher the SFI, the earlier the high bands open and the later they close;
  • Kp tells you whether the geomagnetic field is quiet. At Kp 0 to 2, conditions are as predicted, and the low bands are at their best. At Kp 4 and above, expect paths that pass near the poles - Europe to the west coast of North America, North America to Asia - to be weak or closed, and look for another route: see Finding an Alternate Route.

Watching for a Storm

Wind and Bz tell you what is coming, a few hours before Kp shows it:

  • a solar wind over 500 km/s often brings a disturbance within a day or two;
  • a Bz that stays negative (southward) for hours, especially with a fast wind, means a storm is starting.

When you see these signs, show the Aurora map (Ionospheric Maps) to watch the oval grow towards the equator. A storm closes the polar paths, but it also brings auroral E on 10 and 6 m to stations near the oval, and good conditions on the paths that cross the equator.

Using the Values in the Predictions

The maps do not use the SFI and Kp shown on the panel. With Use STORM Model ticked in Tools > Ionospheric Parameters..., the maps follow geomagnetic disturbances by themselves from the Ap index. To see how the bands would look at a different solar activity, enter a sunspot number of your own in the same dialog; see Ionospheric Maps.

Is the Value Current?

Hover over the panel to see how old each value is. A dash means the program has no value recent enough to trust - over 1 hour old for the wind and Bz, over 36 hours for the SFI and Kp - usually because there is no Internet connection. The values are downloaded only while the panel is shown.

The panel is shown on one map at a time: ticking it under the other map's Ionosphere button moves it there.
